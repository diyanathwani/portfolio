01 / THE BRIEF

Turn product information into a reason to buy.

The paid-ad assignment called for direct-conversion copy for CultStore equipment. This case study focuses on the two pull-up-bar carousels, not the wider equipment brief.

The audience was adults aged 25-35 with an active lifestyle. Working professionals and beginners belonged in that audience; children did not. The voice needed to be short, punchy, playful and motivating, without drifting into recovery or rehabilitation claims.

What the copy had to do

  • Lead with the user's benefit, not a specification list.
  • Make the product's uses clear at a glance.
  • Keep the bar and one shopping action at the centre.
  • Avoid invented ratings, counts and performance results.

My contribution was strategy, copy and imagery direction. The visuals below are presentation mockups, not evidence that the ads ran.

02 / RESEARCH AND BRAND VOICE

Study the pattern. Do not reproduce the ad.

I reviewed CultStore's supplied ad references for their hooks, sentence length, product framing and calls to action.

What I took from the study

Lead with a useful outcome. Use short linked sentences. Show the equipment in use so the reader understands it. Give every card its own reason to keep reading.

What I did not transfer

Massage-chair relaxation language did not belong in a strength-training ad. Ratings, delivery promises and load claims from other products could not be carried across. The reference was a writing study, not permission to reuse every claim.

Visible examples, not proven winners. The supplied library used an impressions-sort parameter, but no impression counts or purchase results were displayed. Also reviewed: 4-in-1 home gym and massage chairs.

03 / COPYWRITING METHOD

From product-centric to user-centric.

A product-centric sentence says what the equipment has. A user-centric sentence says what that feature lets someone do. I kept the detail when it added proof, but led with the activity or benefit.

04 / EXPLORATION TO SELECTION

Give the two routes different jobs.

Exploration covered exercise variety, beginner progression, strength goals, setup and problem-led hooks. I then separated the two selected routes so they did not repeat the same exercise catalogue.

CAROUSEL 1 / PRODUCT EDUCATION

Show what the bar enables.

Group exercises by mounting height, then introduce the selected add-ons. Variety leads. Progression supports the story rather than becoming the whole idea.

CAROUSEL 2 / CUSTOMER EVIDENCE

Answer the trust question.

Use experiences to address stability, installation, removal and grip. Lead with CultStore's own review evidence instead of a marketplace-logo comparison.

This consideration-to-purchase structure is the strategy applied to the work, not a claim about a measured client funnel.

05 / EDITING AND VISUAL DIRECTION

Make every line and image earn its place.

EXPLORATION

Broader exercise libraries, more accessories and a numerical count hook.

SELECTION

Core positions, then band, tube kit and ankle weights. The final cover leads with "1 bar. Endless exercises." and presents the exercise range together.

VISUAL SIMPLIFICATION

Callout circles and competing elements pulled attention away from the exercise.

FINAL DIRECTION

Give scenes more space. Keep movement labels clear. Remove bubbles from exercise cards; use circles on the overview cover only.

VOICE ALIGNMENT

Feature descriptions and formal labels did not match the conversational brief.

FINAL DIRECTION

Use short linked clauses and movement-rooted language: "Lower the bar. Not the effort." The image explains the move; the line gives it energy.

REVIEW ROUTE

The earlier review route included a longer sequence and marketplace comparisons.

FINAL DIRECTION

Six distinct customer experiences, each tied to a buying question. Keep the quote as evidence and the headline short.

The imagery direction used consistent exercise scenes, clear product positions, limited colour accents and strong type contrast. Quieter testimonial layouts gave the customer wording more weight.
