The note.

This campaign makes the note box on a food order a two-way conversation, where the delivery partner writes back and the reply is printed on a sticker on the bag.

01Brand ki samajh

A brand whose voice is already funny, fast and very Indian.

Zomato talks to its customers in a witty, conversational voice, with push notifications and outdoor ads that read like a friend texting rather than a company announcing something.

That makes it the right brand for an idea built on a human line in a small box, because the tone of voice already exists and only the ritual is missing.

This is an independent spec concept, made without Zomato's involvement.

02Observations

What we notice at 10 PM on any ordering app.

  • People type lines like "bell mat bajana, baby soyi hai" in the note box, and those lines are warm, specific and a little funny.
  • Almost nobody ever hears back, so the note box feels like writing into a void.
  • Delivery apps look and feel the same at that hour, which means the only thing that differentiates them is whether the order feels human.

These are strategist observations to be validated with order-note data, and they are not Zomato data.

03Insight

From observation to insight.

People who order food alone at night feel unseen, because the order is a transaction that nobody acknowledges. So Zomato can answer the one honest line a customer writes, and turn that answer into the most shareable thing on the bag.

05 The idea as a six-piece brief

Six sentences. One idea.

Business problem

Food delivery apps look the same at 10 PM, and customer loyalty to any one of them is thin.

Consumer problem

Ordering feels like a transaction, and nobody on the other end seems to see the customer.

Insight

People write kinder lines to a stranger in the note box than to almost anyone else.

Solution

Note Back lets the rider reply with one tap, and the note and reply print as a sticker on the bag.

Consumer goal

The customer wants to feel that a real human is on the other end of the order.

Business goal

Zomato wants the note box to become a ritual that people open on purpose.

06Depth

The strategy under the idea, in plain agency terms.

ICPUrban customers aged 18 to 30 who order for one or two people at night, typically from hostels, shared flats and early-career homes.
Not forBulk and office orders, because the note box is a personal moment.
Hook"Type one line tonight. Your rider will write back."
Rider realityThe rider taps one of six preset reply cards and never types, so the idea adds no time or risk on a bike.
Earned mediaThe sticker is the product, and every peeled and posted sticker is an organic ad on the customer's own story.
DropsA limited weekly Note Drop of seven notes keeps the feed fresh and gives people a reason to check back.
RetentionCustomers return to see whether their note is chosen as the Note of the Week, which is named on a partner restaurant menu.
Go to marketTwo pilot cities with no paid media, then creators, then all cities with a short film cut from real replies.
One call to actionType one line in the note box tonight.
Goal and timelineA 10-week plan with a target of 1 in 10 pilot-city orders carrying a note by week 8, to be set against Zomato's own baseline.
