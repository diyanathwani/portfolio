This campaign turns every college campus into a public talent hunt, where students scan a poster, post a 15-second video and climb a leaderboard for their college.
01Brand ki samajh

A discovery brand that wants to be the first stop for new talent.

Stars N Celebs works on the supply side of the creator economy, which means finding emerging faces and connecting them with brands that need them.

The brand's voice should feel like the campus itself: loud, competitive, funny and a little rebellious, rather than corporate and polite.

Find the Faces is right for this brand because it makes discovery the campaign itself, so every scan produces both an audience and a pipeline of talent.

02Observations

What we see on campuses every single day.

  • Almost every campus has one person everyone already calls a star, and that fame stops at the college gate.
  • Students watch reels all day, yet most of them never post their own because they fear nobody will notice.
  • Rivalry between colleges is the strongest social currency on campus, since fests, sports and inter-college events run on pride.

These are strategist observations to be validated in pilot research, not survey results.

03Insight

From observation to insight.

Students who want to be seen feel stuck inside their campus, because college gives them a stage but never a career. So Stars N Celebs can make a win visible to the whole campus first, and let pride push them outside the gate.

05 The idea as a six-piece brief.

Business problem

Brands pay for reach, while new creators have no clear way in to those brands.

Consumer problem

A student can be famous on campus and still be unknown outside it.

Insight

College builds creators but it does not build careers.

Solution

A public campus hunt in which students scan, drop a video, rank for their college and share a found card.

Consumer goal

The student wants to be seen outside the campus gate.

Business goal

Stars N Celebs wants to be the first discovery step for new creators.

06Depth

The strategy under the idea, in plain agency terms.

ICPCollege students aged 18 to 24 who already make or want to make short video, in campuses with strong fest and inter-college culture.
Not forEstablished influencers, because the hunt exists for people who have not yet been found.
Hook"Your college has a star. Don't let their college beat yours."
Reach planPosters in canteens, libraries and hostels, then college meme pages, then student-run clubs as local captains.
Earned mediaEvery found card is a shareable asset, so winners spread the campaign through their own stories.
ScarcityEach campus gets a limited number of found cards every week, which keeps entry competitive and gives a reason to return.
RetentionA weekly leaderboard deadline lets colleges defend their rank, so students come back to drop again.
Brand partnerOne brand partner per campus tribe, such as music, dance or comedy, who offers the weekly prize and gets first sight of the talent.
Go to marketPilot on 50 campuses, switch on rivalry leaderboards, then open brand briefs to the top creators.
One call to actionScan the poster and drop your 15 seconds.
Goal and timelineA 10-week pilot, with targets for scans and creator enquiries set before launch.
