01Brand ki samajh

A project that sells rooted, family-first living in Nagpur.

The project positions itself around the line "Rooted in memories. Ready for what's next," so the brand already speaks the language of family, belonging and continuity rather than the language of square feet and discounts.

Its voice is warm and grounded, and its buyer is a Nagpur family weighing one of the biggest decisions of its life with a lot of emotion and a lot of caution.

This campaign suits the brand because it turns the existing line into something a family can physically do, instead of something a hoarding can only say.

Brand model
02Observations

Three human truths we noticed before writing a single line.

  • Most Indian families keep one old photo in a drawer or an album, and it is usually taken against a wall in the home they grew up in.
  • Many families keep a spot in the house where children's heights were marked in pencil, and people leave it untouched even when they repaint everything else.
  • When a family visits a site office, the conversation is about price and possession dates, and the emotional question of whether this will feel like home is never asked out loud.

These are strategist observations to be validated in launch-stage research with Nagpur families, and they are not survey results.

03Insight

The leap from observation to insight.

Families who are about to buy a new home feel a quiet grief about leaving the old one, because the memories live in the walls and the new house has none yet. So the project can become the place that gives them their first memory before they even move in.

In short, people do not move homes, they move stories.

Insight model
first memory

In the old house, a child stood against the wall every birthday while a parent drew a pencil line above their head.

Year after year the lines climbed, and the wall quietly kept the score of a whole childhood.

Then the family bought a new home, and the old wall had to stay behind in a box of memories.

The new walls were clean and beautiful, but they had no pencil marks, no stains and no stories yet.

So we ask the family to dig out one old photo and stand in the same pose in front of the new wall.

The flash goes off, the photo goes up, and the new house finally has its first memory.

Customer journey
05 The idea, as a six-piece brief

Six sentences that hold the whole idea.

1 Business problem

A housing project in Nagpur needs more family visits to turn into bookings, and it needs about 5 homes sold to break even (projected).

2 Consumer problem

Buying a new home feels like leaving the old one behind, because the old house holds the memories and the new one is bare walls.

3 Insight

People do not move homes, they move stories.

4 Solution

Families recreate one old family photo inside the new home and share it as Walls That Raised Us.

5 Consumer goal

The family wants to know that the new house can feel like home before they move in.

6 Business goal

The project wants two to three times more site-office visits (projected) and a faster yes or no.

06Depth

The strategy under the idea, in plain agency terms.

ICPNagpur families aged roughly 28 to 45, upgrading from a rented or older home, with children, and with grandparents who influence the decision.
Not forPure investors who never plan to live in the home, because the idea works on belonging and a family has to be present to take part.
Hook"Bring one old family photo and we will help you recreate it in your future home."
Reach planLocal Nagpur creators and family-run community pages first, then geo-targeted social to the same neighbourhoods, then site-office walk-ins.
Earned mediaThe recreated photos are the content, so each family that posts becomes a distribution channel without paid support.
RetentionAfter purchase each family takes a first-night photo, and that photo is added to the memory wall as the next chapter.
Go to marketSeed with ten families, open to every visitor, then run it as the standing hook for each new tower launch.
Physical objectA pencil-mark height chart or Polaroid frame is hand-delivered to shortlisted families so the idea arrives as something they can hold.
One call to actionBook a site visit and bring your photo.
Goal and timelineA first 90-day pilot with ten families, with targets for tags and visits set before launch.
The fear behind the idea

Every buyer quietly fears leaving their memories behind.

We answer that fear first, because a family that feels safe about its memories is a family that is ready to say yes.
