first memory

In the old house, a child stood against the wall every birthday while a parent drew a pencil line above their head.

Year after year the lines climbed, and the wall quietly kept the score of a whole childhood.

Then the family bought a new home, and the old wall had to stay behind in a box of memories.

The new walls were clean and beautiful, but they had no pencil marks, no stains and no stories yet.

So we ask the family to dig out one old photo and stand in the same pose in front of the new wall.

The flash goes off, the photo goes up, and the new house finally has its first memory.

The brief on one page

Six sentences that hold the whole idea.

1 Business problem

A housing project in Nagpur needs more family visits to turn into bookings, and it needs about 5 homes sold to break even (projected).

2 Consumer problem

Buying a new home feels like leaving the old one behind, because the old house holds the memories and the new one is bare walls.

3 Insight

People do not move homes, they move stories.

4 Solution

Families recreate one old family photo inside the new home and share it as Walls That Raised Us.

5 Consumer goal

The family wants to know that the new house can feel like home before they move in.

6 Business goal

The project wants two to three times more site-office visits (projected) and a faster yes or no.

The fear behind the idea

Every buyer quietly fears leaving their memories behind.

We answer that fear first, because a family that feels safe about its memories is a family that is ready to say yes.
