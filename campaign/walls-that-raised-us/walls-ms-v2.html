01 · Brand ki samajh

A project that sells rooted, family-first living in Nagpur.

The project positions itself around "Rooted in memories. Ready for what's next," so the brand already speaks the language of family, belonging and continuity rather than square feet and discounts.

Its voice is warm and grounded, and its buyer is a Nagpur family weighing one of the biggest decisions of its life with a lot of emotion and a lot of caution.

This campaign suits the brand because it turns the existing line into something a family can physically do, instead of something a hoarding can only say.

Brand model. Four pillars carry the line: memory, family, proof and place.

02 · Observations

Three human truths we noticed before writing a line.

1Most Indian families keep one old photo in a drawer or an album, and it is usually taken against a wall in the home they grew up in.
2Many families keep a spot where children's heights were marked in pencil, and leave it untouched even when they repaint everything else.
3At a site office the talk is price and possession dates, and the emotional question of whether this will feel like home is never asked aloud.
Sourcing
  1. Observations 1 to 3: strategist observations from everyday Indian family life. They are not survey results and are marked for validation.
  2. Validation plan: a short round of interviews with Nagpur families who recently moved, before launch.
03 · Insight

Families about to buy feel a quiet grief about leaving the old home, because the memories live in the walls and the new house has none yet. So the project can give them their first memory before they move in.

People do not move homes. They move stories.

Sourcing
  1. Insight built from observations 1 to 3 in the Cole format: people who [audience] feel [tension], because [reason]. So [brand] can [opportunity].

04 · Story

A pencil mark, a flash, and a first memory.

1

In the old house a child stood against the wall every birthday while a parent drew a pencil line above their head.

2

Year after year the lines climbed, and the wall quietly kept the score of a whole childhood.

3

Then the family bought a new home, and the old wall stayed behind in a box of memories.

4

The new walls were clean and beautiful, but they had no pencil marks and no stories yet.

5

So the family digs out one old photo and stands in the same pose in front of the new wall.

6

The flash goes off, the photo goes up, and the new house finally has its first memory.

05 · The idea, as a six-piece brief

Recreate One Memory.

1Business problem

A housing project in Nagpur needs more family visits to turn into bookings, and about 5 homes sold to break even (projected).

2Consumer problem

Buying a new home feels like leaving the old one behind, because the old house holds the memories and the new one is bare walls.

3Insight

People do not move homes, they move stories.

4Solution

Families recreate one old photo inside the new home and share it as Walls That Raised Us.

5Consumer goal

The family wants to know the new house can feel like home before they move in.

6Business goal

Two to three times more site-office visits (projected) and a faster yes or no.

06 · Depth

The strategy under the idea, in agency terms.

ICP

Nagpur families aged roughly 28 to 45, upgrading from a rented or older home, with children, and grandparents who influence the decision.

Not for

Pure investors who never plan to live in the home, because the idea needs a family present to take part.

Hook

"Bring one old family photo and we will help you recreate it in your future home."

Reach plan

Local Nagpur creators and family-run community pages first, then geo-targeted social to the same neighbourhoods, then site-office walk-ins.

Earned media

The recreated photos are the content, so each family that posts becomes a distribution channel without paid support.

Retention

After purchase each family takes a first-night photo, added to the memory wall as the next chapter.

Go to market

Seed with ten families, open to every visitor, then run it as the standing hook for each new tower launch.

Physical object

A pencil-mark height chart or Polaroid frame, hand-delivered to shortlisted families.

One CTA and timeline

Book a site visit and bring your photo. A 90-day pilot with ten families, targets set before launch.

Challenges and what could go wrong

What we would watch from day one.

  • Some families will not have an old photo, so the site office keeps a scanned-album service on hand.
  • Recreated photos may feel staged, so we ask for the same pose and nothing more.
  • Privacy comes first: no photo goes on the memory wall or online without the family's say-so.
  • Ten families is a small start, so we read the pilot as a learning round, not a verdict.
