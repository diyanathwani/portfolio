01 / Understand · the brand and the category

What the category does, and what it leaves open.

Before any concept, I read Cultstore's live ads in the Meta Ad Library and scanned the category's active ads in India.

Cultstore's own ads

The pattern to respect.

  • Hook: one line, one idea. A pain ("sore after leg day?") or an identity line.
  • No more than three concrete proofs: "no-drill", the load rating, "foam grip". Never "premium quality".
  • The ending: offer code, free delivery, Shop Now.
  • Carousels: emoji as bullets, sentence case, caps only for one word.
  • Massager video: pain question, relief claim, three features fast, then price and CTA.
How I studied them

The pattern, never a copy.

  • Live ads pulled from the Meta Ad Library, sorted by impressions.
  • The winning pattern: problem or benefit hook, product, two or three concrete features, exact offer, Shop Now.
  • Individual ads flagged as pattern references. The pattern, never a copy.
  • Prices and ratings taken from cultstore.com, and real customer reviews quoted verbatim.
The category's ads

What every doorway-bar brand says.

  • Doorway transformation, no-drill, exercise variety and space-saving are the shared language.
  • So none of them can be the unique claim.
  • Common formats: a short outcome hook on the first frame, a creator demo in a real home, 20 to 30 second videos, one benefit per ad, a simple Shop Now.
  • Adjacent equipment brands lead with clutter: a rack of dumbbells replaced by one set.
What worked

Carry these forward.

  • A short outcome hook on frame one.
  • A creator demo in a real home setting.
  • One clear benefit per ad.
  • A simple Shop Now.
What did not fit

Leave these out.

  • A family or kids angle: not the audience.
  • Price urgency alone: against a premium positioning.
  • A dry feature checklist alone: generic, and the swipe stops.
  • Generated scenes sold as proof, fake urgency, or calling an ad a winner without sales data.
In the Meta Ad Library

Cultstore's live ads.

What I did with it

The gap, and three decisions.

The open gap

Every pull-up ad from the brand was feature-led. The emotional "first pull-up" angle was empty.

Features support

The capacity checklist went into support lines, never the hero.

No comparison card

A "best bar" claim could not be defended. The load rating is not unique in the category and there was no independent testing. Positioned on value and features instead.

Concepts picked

The two least duplicated routes had a human angle no pull-up ad used yet.

Tested directions

Show equipment in a real home and routine. Open on one buyer goal, not everything at once: a published retailer test found interest-tailored video lifted ROAS 51% over generic. Keep refreshing real product demos.

My recommendation

A short real-home setup-and-use video: one objection, one real demo. Stronger to test than an exercise-list carousel.

04.1 / The copy playbook

The rules every card followed.

Written down after round one, and carried into round two.

Working direction

A paid-ads copywriter's brief.

  • Written as a fitness paid-ads copywriter on Meta formats, conversion first.
  • Audience 25 to 35, active lifestyle, beginners included.
  • Short, punchy, quirky, motivating, challenging.
  • Research-backed, no over-claiming. User-centric claims over spec dumps.
  • Keywords gym-goers actually search.
Copy rules

Sharp, honest, one job a line.

  • The product is the hero on every card. An add-on earns a card only if it adds exercises on the bar, and only Cult Store SKUs.
  • One job per line. No two lines doing the same work.
  • Register is a spectrum: direct, funny, witty, satirical. Wordplay is never forced.
  • Every carousel is distinct, and the opener matches its own theme.
Claims

Real, or not at all.

  • Real published reviews, verbatim, with names and ratings.
  • Live prices and ratings from cultstore.com, so traffic goes to the brand store.
  • No medical or pain-relief claims. Framed as strength and benefit instead.
  • Product named exactly as on the store. Title Case for add-ons.
Client rules

Evergreen and brand-first.

  • No festive or seasonal elements.
  • No other marketplaces' logos or names. Only the brand's own store rating.
  • Implement what the client asked. Push back once with an alternative, then follow the client.
Design direction

Imagery directed card by card.

Same model, full photos

One model across a set, full-size uncut photos, and the brand's official colours.

Exercise-first images

Exercise names inside the image, with the targeted muscles as a soft glow, not circles.

Reviews as chats

Testimonials styled as real chat bubbles, with flat highlight bands on one to three key words.

A real CTA slide

The last card reads as a call to action, not another content card.

04.2 / The final copy

Carousel 1, card by card.

One header, one tag, one benefit line per card.

Card 1 · Cover

One question, one answer.

  • "No room for a home gym? We've got your back. (Quite literally!)"
  • "1 bar. Endless exercises."
Cards 2 to 4 · Mount heights

One bar, three positions.

  • Door-top: "Hang out at home. Your muscles won't get to relax."
  • Waist level: "Ground yourself to lift better." Your doorway can now be a row machine.
  • Floor level: "Lower the bar. Not the effort." Floor mounting levels up your push-ups.
Cards 5 to 7 · Add-ons

Each one multiplies the moves.

  • Resistance band: "Add a band. Multiply the moves." The band shares your weight till you don't need it.
  • Tube kit: "More directions. Stronger back." Face pulls fix the posture your desk job ruined.
  • Ankle weights: "When hanging gets easy, add weight." A little weight on the ankles, a lot more on the core.
Carousel 2 · Reviews

Customers carry the story.

  • Hook: "ATTENTION! The reviews are IN. We have set the bar HIGH."
  • "Bye bye, wobble!" for stability. "Let dead hangs make you feel alive." for build quality.
  • "No drilling required. No excuses allowed." for installation.
  • "Take down the bar, but keep up your habits." for removal. "Firm hold. Yet, the softest touch." for the foam grips.
  • All six are real store reviews, quoted verbatim.
05 / Client rounds

Twelve comments. All worked in.

The client reviewed both carousels on the working doc. Every comment was implemented and synced across the doc and the image folders.

Carousel 1 · 8 comments

Less crowded. More direct.

  • Cover: too crowded. Kept one question and two lines.
  • Cards 2 to 4: tags added, subheadings removed.
  • Card 5: reframed around versatility.
  • Card 7: reframed around progression.
  • Length: seven cards is right. Cards 8 to 12 removed.
Carousel 2 · 4 comments

Crisp. Evergreen. Brand-first.

  • Cover: one great hook. Festive elements and other platforms' logos removed, so ads can run all year and drive sales to the store.
  • Copy: trimmed so the review is the focus.
  • Length: one hook and six testimonials. One card removed.
  • Cover line: "Pssst, see what others have to say about it."
Why each change mattered

What the client was steering toward.

Fewer slides

The client wanted tighter carousels. Seven cards for the library, one hook and six testimonials for the reviews. Cards 8 to 12 and one testimonial came out.

Regular posting

The festival offer and dates were removed so the carousels work in regular posting, not only in a sale window.

More imaginable

A fixed exercise count would have capped the idea and needed more slides. "Endless" lets the viewer picture their own routine.

Bar first

The pull-up bar stays the hero. Add-ons stay only where they add exercises on the bar.

Harder moves

The cards climb in difficulty, ending on weighted hanging raises, instead of repeating beginner moves.

Brand store first

Other platforms' logos and ratings came out so every ad drives sales to the Cultstore itself.

01 / Briefing

The routine, not the recovery.

  • Angle: "Don't wait till you're Sore." Make muscle care part of the routine: before training, between workouts, after hard sessions.
  • Deliverables: carousel and video.
  • Marketed as a category. The price shown is "Starting from Rs 799", never a single model.
02 / Applying round one

Fewer words, first time right.

  • Less, uncongested copy: two lines per card, with image tags for the design team.
  • Concepts pitched as a blocker solved and what each slide shows.
  • Real claims only. Specs checked across models: one button, 4 heads, 6 speeds, Type-C.
03 / The carousel

Excuse Audit.

  • A hero, then one objection per card: does it even work, too complicated, too expensive, too bulky, no time, not for me.
  • Each card shows its own claim: a physio bills every visit, Cult bills you once. The remote has more buttons.
  • A Graphite theme, picked from white, cream and graphite options.
