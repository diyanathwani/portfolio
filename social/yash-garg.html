01.2 / Understand · his voice

Written to sound like him.

I mapped his past videos before writing a line. The script follows what already works for his audience.

Persona

The senior bhaiya.

  • Someone who has seen the system and wants to save students from bad decisions.
  • Practical and outcome-focused. A little dramatic, never fake.
  • Addresses the viewer as "tum" and "bhai". Never "sir", never "teacher".
Hooks

Three kinds, used consistently.

  • Shock number: the fee, the package.
  • Small gap, big outcome: a few questions that change the college.
  • Student problem: a DM from a viewer.
Rhythm

Zig-zag, not linear.

  • Point, example, money impact, a joke, then back to the point.
  • Informal transitions: "ab problem yahan aati hai", "lekin asli baat suno".
  • Key lines repeated in different words for emphasis.
Trust

Case studies and soft CTAs.

  • A friend, a student DM, a personal story or a past result.
  • Humour is self-deprecating or lightly sarcastic, and short.
  • Subscribe asks sit mid-video, the comment ask at the end.
The emotional arc

Shock → fear → hope → steps → reassurance → verdict.

Most of his videos follow this arc, which is why they feel like mentoring. The scripts keep it, and add hard data inside it.

03 / The chosen YouTube strategy

Sixteen layers, in four groups.

Attention

Earn the first 20 seconds.

  • Hook: fear, curiosity and money pressure together.
  • Stereotype break: acknowledge what people say, then break it.
  • Psychology: hit the five fears.
  • Blunt honesty: a trust-building tone.
Story + proof

Make it feel researched.

  • Story framework: a student's decision journey.
  • Data + visuals: proof on screen for every claim.
  • Distribution: salary spread, not one number.
  • Insider detail: small facts that show real research.
Decision

Turn a review into a tool.

  • Choice consequence: the good path and the bad path.
  • Fantasy vs reality: freedom is also a distraction risk.
  • Decision tree: who should choose it, and who should avoid it.
  • Warning + mentor: the problem is the misuse of freedom.
  • Clear verdict: a specific recommendation.
Authority + growth

Build the channel, too.

  • Alumni authority: the global network, used lightly.
  • Future opportunity: startups, exchange and international exposure.
  • Cross-promotion: a natural pointer to the next related video.
Hook + myth-breaking + hard data + story + psychology + mentor advice + a clear decision.
03.1 / The strategy in the script

What each layer looks like.

The stack is a set of moves with a shape. These are the ones I wrote the Manipal script around, each with the reason behind it.

The hook

Big claim, doubt, curiosity.

  • Open on the money: the fee against the median salary.
  • Raise the doubt: is it the smartest decision or an expensive trap?
  • Why: the viewer needs the answer, so they stay.
The stereotype break

Acknowledge, break, explain.

  • Name what people already say about the college: party, freedom, rich kids.
  • Say the real story is different.
  • Why: the break only lands if the data earns it after.
The distribution

The real placement story.

  • Where most creators show the highest package, the script shows the spread: top 50, top 100, top 250 and the overall median.
  • Why: the gap between the headline and the median is the story.
Choice consequence

Two paths, one college.

  • The bad path: party, low CGPA, low placement.
  • The good path: clubs, coding, internships, top companies.
  • Why: the video stops reviewing and starts mentoring.
The decision tree

Categories, not a verdict on the college.

  • Choose it if: the alternative is a tier 3 NIT, you want startups, you want international exposure.
  • Avoid it if: the branch is low, the family depends on a loan, or self-discipline is weak.
Mentor reframe

The problem is not the college.

  • Warn without judging: the problem is the misuse of freedom.
  • Blunt honesty builds trust: freedom without discipline can derail a career.
  • Why: end on one specific recommendation, never a vague one.
Retention and channel growth

The close points to the next video.

The final move is a natural pointer to the related College vs Branch video, so one video feeds the next and the channel's watch time grows.

05 / Creator growth · beyond one video

A decision system, not an opinion.

For the follow-up video, I built a framework that settles IIT vs NIT vs IIIT vs BITS and branch dilemmas without special-case explanations.

College tiers
Tier 1Legacy IITs: Bombay, Delhi, Madras, Kanpur, Kharagpur, Roorkee, Guwahati.
Tier 2Upper IITs, top NITs, IIIT Hyderabad and BITS Pilani main campus.
Tier 3Remaining NITs, DTU, NSUT, PEC, IIIT Delhi and Bangalore, BIT Mesra, COEP, VJTI, Jadavpur, Anna CEG.
Tier 4Mid NIT, IIIT and strong regional colleges.
Tier 5Remaining private colleges.
Branch tiers
ACSE, IT, MnC, AI.
BECE, EE.
CMechanical, Chemical.
DCivil, Metallurgy.
The gap
College gap: 2+ tiers is big, 1 is moderate, same tier is small.
Branch gap: a 2-level difference is big.
Decision logic

Read the two gaps together.

Big college gap + small branch gapCollege
Small college gap + big branch gapBranch
Both bigIntent-driven
Both smallMedian + location
Three filters

Before the answer is final.

  • Financial safety: can the family absorb the loan at the median salary?
  • Intent: tech, MBA, research or startup.
  • Worst case: if coding fails, the market slows, interest changes or health slips, which option survives?
Scoring + rule

Ten criteria, one rule.

  • Scored 1 to 5: financial safety, placement stability, personal fit, worst-case safety, growth horizon, AI resilience, curriculum flexibility, communication, burnout tolerance, institutional stability.
  • Tie-breaker: lower financial risk wins.
  • Rule: financial floor × structural ceiling × personal reality.
